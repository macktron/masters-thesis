\chapter*{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}

Passive electronic support measures record a time-ordered stream of pulse descriptor words in which several radars are interleaved and each radar may change its operating mode.
Operators need both emitter and mode groupings from the same stream.
Classical pipelines deinterleave first and then classify modes, so the two partitions are never formed jointly from the mixed list.

The experiments use a synthetic, fully labelled scenario corpus.
Each pulse carries a recording-local emitter identity and a mode from a catalogue of $19$ types shared across recordings.
Training, validation and test windows contain $2000$ pulses, created by breaking up recordings of different simulated scenarios into fixed windows.
Scores are reported on $1169$ non-overlapping test windows.

A transformer encoder with a shared trunk and two separate branches, one for deinterleaving and one for mode clustering, is trained with two supervised contrastive losses.
The losses differ only in how positives are chosen.
The emitter loss treats pulses from the same recording-local emitter as positives.
The mode loss treats pulses with the same global mode label as positives.

At inference time, density-based clustering (DBSCAN) is applied independently to the embeddings of each branch.

Standard self-attention already recovers both partitions on most windows.
Mean emitter adjusted Rand index (ARI) is $0.948$ and mean mode ARI is $0.985$.
Rotary encoding of time of arrival and a pairwise physical attention bias raise emitter ARI to $1.000$ and remove the remaining failure tail.
A Vanilla encoder trained only for deinterleaving outperforms the joint model on emitters (ARI $0.999$ versus $0.948$), while a mode-only encoder reaches a similar level on modes (ARI $0.983$).
The joint objective is therefore a trade-off, not a free extra head.
Held-out modulations and operational recordings are left to future work.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} transformer, deep clustering, electronic intelligence, radar emitter identification, mode recognition, supervised contrastive learning.
