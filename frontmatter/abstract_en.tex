\chapter*{Abstract}
\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}
Passive electronic support measures record a time-ordered stream of
pulse descriptor words in which several radars are interleaved and
each radar may change its operating mode. Operators need both
groupings from the same stream. Classical pipelines deinterleave
first and then classify modes. Performing the two tasks in parallel
rather than sequentially could supply more information to mode
classification and, in a machine learning model, improve
representation learning.

The experiments use a synthetic, fully labeled scenario corpus. Each
pulse carries a recording-local emitter identity and a mode from a
catalog of 17 types shared across recordings. Training, validation,
and test windows contain 2000 pulses, obtained by splitting
recordings from different simulated scenarios into fixed windows.
Scores are reported on 1168 non-overlapping test windows.

A transformer encoder with a shared trunk and two separate branches,
one for deinterleaving and one for mode clustering, is trained with
supervised contrastive losses aggregated from both branches. At
inference time, density-based clustering (DBSCAN) is applied
independently to the embeddings of each branch.

Standard self-attention already recovers both partitions on most
windows (mean emitter adjusted Rand index, ARI, 0.948; mode ARI
0.985). Rotary encoding of time of arrival and a pairwise physical
attention bias raise emitter ARI to 1.000 and remove the remaining
failure tail. A vanilla encoder trained only for deinterleaving
outperforms the joint model on emitters (ARI 0.999 versus 0.948),
while a mode-only encoder matches the joint mode scores (ARI 0.983).
The joint objective is therefore a trade-off, not a cost-free extra
head. Held-out modulations and operational recordings are left to
future work.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} transformer, deep clustering, electronic intelligence, radar emitter identification, mode recognition, supervised contrastive learning.
