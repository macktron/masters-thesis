\section{Baseline Methods}\label{sec:method:baselines}

The comparisons isolate the contribution of $\Loss_{\mathrm{md}}$ in \cref{eq:method:loss_total} from that of the attention variants in \cref{sec:method:attention}, relative to standard self-attention.
Final configurations and numbers are deferred to \cref{ch:experiments}; this section fixes only the design intent of each control.

\begin{description}
  \item[Dual-branch, single-task.] The encoder of \cref{sec:method:arch} with standard self-attention, trained with one contrastive term only: $\Loss_{\mathrm{em}}$ (emitter-only) or $\Loss_{\mathrm{md}}$ (mode-only).
  Architecture, width, and decoding remain those of the joint model, so the only change is the objective.
  Emitter-only is the comparable transformer for whether the joint loss outperforms deinterleaving-alone training.
  Mode-only is the symmetric control, trained only on $\Loss_{\mathrm{md}}$.
  Both branches are still decoded with \gls{dbscan}, including the branch that received no direct gradient.

  \item[\Gls{dbscan} on fixed window features.] No transformer; \gls{dbscan} \parencite{ester1996density} clusters a deterministic vector built from the scaled \glspl{pdw} of a window.
  There is no training.
  The neighbourhood radius $\varepsilon$ is selected by grid search on the validation set, holding $\mathrm{minPts}$ fixed; the selected $\varepsilon$ is then frozen for the held-out test evaluation.
  Separates the gain from learned sequence context from what density clustering already recovers on hand-specified summaries.
\end{description}
