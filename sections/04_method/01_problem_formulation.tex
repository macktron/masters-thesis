\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:method:formulation}

\subsection*{Observed input}

The data consist of $S$ pulse trains indexed by $s \in \{1,\ldots,S\}$.
Pulse train $s$ is observed as a finite, time-ordered sequence of \glspl{pdw}
\[
  \mat{X}^{(s)} = (\vect{x}^{(s)}_1,\ldots,\vect{x}^{(s)}_{N_s}),
  \qquad
  \vect{x}^{(s)}_n \in \R^{d},
\]
where each $\vect{x}^{(s)}_n$ contains the measured parameters of one pulse (carrier frequency, amplitude, pulse width, time of arrival, and angles of arrival), and the length $N_s$ varies between pulse trains.
The encoder operates on fixed-length windows extracted from these trains as described in \cref{sec:method:data:window}.
When considering one train or window, the superscript is omitted.

\subsection*{Labels and target partitions}

Each pulse $n \in \{1,\ldots,N_s\}$ carries an emitter identity $e^{(s)}_n \in \{1,\ldots,K^{(s)}\}$ and an operating mode $m^{(s)}_n \in \{1,\ldots,M\}$.
The emitter count $K^{(s)}$ may vary between pulse trains, and each emitter identifier is meaningful only within its train.
Mode labels, by contrast, are drawn from a finite catalogue of size $M$ shared across all pulse trains.
A train generally contains only $M^{(s)} \leq M$ of these modes.
The two labels describe different relations: one emitter may switch between several modes, and the same mode may occur on several emitters.

For pulse train $s$, the labels induce emitter and mode cells on the pulse-index set,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:emitter_partition}
  \mathcal{E}^{(s)}_k = \{\, n : e^{(s)}_n = k \,\},
  \qquad
  k \in \{1,\ldots,K^{(s)}\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:mode_partition}
  \mathcal{M}^{(s)}_\ell = \{\, n : m^{(s)}_n = \ell \,\},
  \qquad
  \ell \in \{1,\ldots,M\}.
\end{equation}
The emitter cells are non-empty and partition $\{1,\ldots,N_s\}$.
The mode cells are also disjoint, but $\mathcal{M}^{(s)}_\ell$ is empty when mode $\ell$ is absent from the train; the non-empty mode cells form the corresponding partition.
Recovering the emitter partition is the deinterleaving task, whereas recovering the mode partition groups pulses by signal behavior irrespective of physical source.

\subsection*{Dual embedding model}

For an input $\mat{X}=(\vect{x}_1,\ldots,\vect{x}_L)$, the parametric encoder produces two embedding sequences,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:dual_embeddings}
  \mat{Z}^{\mathrm{em}} = (\vect{z}^{\mathrm{em}}_1,\ldots,\vect{z}^{\mathrm{em}}_L),
  \quad
  \vect{z}^{\mathrm{em}}_n \in \R^{p},
  \qquad
  \mat{Z}^{\mathrm{md}} = (\vect{z}^{\mathrm{md}}_1,\ldots,\vect{z}^{\mathrm{md}}_L),
  \quad
  \vect{z}^{\mathrm{md}}_n \in \R^{q}.
\end{equation}
The emitter branch represents similarity by physical source, while the mode branch represents similarity by operating mode.
Their detailed architecture is given in \cref{sec:method:arch}.

\subsection*{Clustering objective}

At inference, only the window $\mat{X}$ is observed; its labels and the numbers of groups present are unknown.
Each embedding sequence is clustered independently to obtain the per-pulse assignments
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:emitter_predictor}
  \hat{e}_n \in \{1,\ldots,\hat{K}\},
  \qquad n \in \{1,\ldots,L\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:mode_predictor}
  \hat{m}_n \in \{1,\ldots,\hat{M}\},
  \qquad n \in \{1,\ldots,L\},
\end{equation}
where $\hat{K}$ and $\hat{M}$ are inferred by the clustering operator.
These assignments induce the estimated partitions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:estimated_partitions}
  \widehat{\mathcal{E}}_k
  =
  \{\,n : \hat{e}_n = k\,\},
  \quad k \in \{1,\ldots,\hat{K}\},
  \qquad
  \widehat{\mathcal{M}}_\ell
  =
  \{\,n : \hat{m}_n = \ell\,\},
  \quad \ell \in \{1,\ldots,\hat{M}\}.
\end{equation}
The joint clustering objective is to recover, within each window, the restrictions of both ground-truth partitions in \cref{eq:method:emitter_partition,eq:method:mode_partition} to that window, up to independent permutations of their cluster indices.
The cluster symbols themselves carry no meaning; agreement depends only on which pulses are grouped together.

Both branches are decoded per window, as formalized in \cref{eq:method:cluster_decoding}.
Emitter identities are therefore interpreted only within the pulse train from which the window was drawn, and the predicted cluster symbols are local to the window.
Mode clusters are also produced locally, but their embedding geometry is made comparable across pulse trains through globally shared mode supervision during training.
Neither $\hat{K}$ nor $\hat{M}$ is supplied to the model, and $\hat{M}$ is not constrained by the training catalogue size $M$; this permits clusters corresponding to modulation types not observed during training.

\subsection*{Learning objective}

Training uses the labelled pulse trains to fit the map $\mat{X} \mapsto (\mat{Z}^{\mathrm{em}},\mat{Z}^{\mathrm{md}})$.
The total objective is the sum of two supervised contrastive losses with the same functional form: emitter labels define positive pairs for $\mat{Z}^{\mathrm{em}}$, while globally shared mode labels define positive pairs for $\mat{Z}^{\mathrm{md}}$.
The complete loss is specified in \cref{sec:method:loss}; the post-hoc clustering step is not part of that optimization.
