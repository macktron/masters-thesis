\section{Attention Variants}\label{sec:method:attention}

The encoder of \cref{sec:method:arch} uses standard self-attention, which treats a window as an unordered set of tokens and lets every pulse attend to every other pulse purely through learned query--key compatibility.
The only ordering or physical-relationship cue available to it is whatever the input features themselves carry, in particular the \gls{toa} entry of each \gls{pdw}.
A radar pulse train, however, has strong pairwise structure: two pulses that are close in time, share a carrier frequency, or have a similar pulse width are far more likely to originate from the same emitter or mode than an arbitrary pair.
This section describes three variants of the attention mechanism that make such structure explicit. Each is evaluated as a drop-in replacement for the \gls{mha} sub-layers of \cref{sec:method:arch}; their relative merit is reported in \cref{ch:experiments}.

Throughout, we write the attention logits of a single head over a window of $L$ pulses as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:attn_base}
  \mat{A} = \frac{\mat{Q}\mat{K}^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}},
  \qquad
  A_{ij} = \frac{\vect{q}_i^{\top}\vect{k}_j}{\sqrt{d_k}},
\end{equation}
where $\vect{q}_i$ and $\vect{k}_j$ are the query and key of pulses $i$ and $j$ and $d_k$ is the per-head dimension.
The attention weights are the row-wise softmax of $\mat{A}$, which then mixes the value vectors.

\subsection*{Basic attention bias}

The first variant adds a handcrafted pairwise bias to the logits,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:attn_bias}
  A_{ij} = \frac{\vect{q}_i^{\top}\vect{k}_j}{\sqrt{d_k}} + B_{ij},
\end{equation}
where $B_{ij}$ is a fixed function of the physical relationship between pulses $i$ and $j$, such as their time separation $|t_i - t_j|$, carrier-frequency difference $|f_i - f_j|$, or pulse-width difference.
Designing $B$ so that physically related pairs receive a larger value pushes attention towards them before any learning takes place, injecting radar-domain knowledge directly into the mechanism.
This mirrors the relative-position and content-independent biases used in language and graph transformers \parencite{shaw2018relative,ying2021graphormer}, specialised here to pulse parameters rather than token indices.

\subsection*{MLP attention bias}

Choosing $B$ by hand fixes in advance which relationships matter and how strongly.
The second variant instead learns the bias: for each pulse pair we form a vector of pairwise features $\vect{\phi}_{ij}$ (the same physical differences as above, and any others worth including) and pass it through a small shared \gls{mlp},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:attn_mlp_bias}
  B_{ij} = \mathrm{MLP}(\vect{\phi}_{ij}),
\end{equation}
which is then inserted into \cref{eq:method:attn_bias} exactly as before.
The network is free to discover which pulse relationships should increase or decrease attention and by how much, rather than committing to a hand-tuned rule.
This generalises the fixed bias of \cref{eq:method:attn_bias} and is the edge-feature analogue of attention used in graph transformers \parencite{ying2021graphormer}.

\subsection*{Rotary positional encoding}

The two bias variants encode \emph{which} pulses are related; the third encodes \emph{where} a pulse lies along one or more physical axes.
\Gls{rope} \parencite{su2024roformer} assigns each pulse a scalar coordinate $p_i$—in the original formulation simply the token index, $p_i = i$—and encodes it as a phase: the query and key of pulse $i$ are rotated by angles proportional to $p_i$ before the dot product of \cref{eq:method:attn_base} is taken.
To do so, the $d_k$ dimensions of a head (assumed even) are grouped into $d_k/2$ two-dimensional planes, and the $m$-th plane is rotated by the angle $p_i\theta_m$ using the planar rotation matrix
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:rope_2d}
  \mat{\Omega}(\phi) =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \cos\phi & -\sin\phi \\
    \sin\phi & \phantom{-}\cos\phi
  \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Collecting the per-plane rotations into a single block-diagonal matrix gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:rope_block}
  \mat{R}(p) = \diag\!\bigl(\mat{\Omega}(p\theta_1),\,\mat{\Omega}(p\theta_2),\,\ldots,\,\mat{\Omega}(p\theta_{d_k/2})\bigr),
  \qquad
  \theta_m = b^{-2(m-1)/d_k},
\end{equation}
with a fixed base $b$ (we use $b = 10^4$, following \textcite{su2024roformer}).
The geometric progression of angular frequencies $\theta_m$ mirrors the wavelengths of the sinusoidal encoding of \textcite{vaswani2017attention}: fast-rotating planes resolve fine coordinate differences, slow-rotating planes capture coarse ones.

The key property of this construction is that composing two planar rotations subtracts their angles, $\mat{\Omega}(\phi)^{\top}\mat{\Omega}(\psi) = \mat{\Omega}(\psi - \phi)$, so block-wise $\mat{R}(p_i)^{\top}\mat{R}(p_j) = \mat{R}(p_j - p_i)$.
The attention logit between the rotated query and key therefore depends only on the coordinate \emph{difference},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:method:rope}
  \tilde{\vect{q}}_i = \mat{R}(p_i)\,\vect{q}_i,
  \qquad
  \tilde{\vect{k}}_j = \mat{R}(p_j)\,\vect{k}_j,
  \qquad
  \tilde{\vect{q}}_i^{\top}\tilde{\vect{k}}_j
    = \vect{q}_i^{\top}\mat{R}(p_j - p_i)\,\vect{k}_j,
\end{equation}
even though each pulse is rotated by its absolute coordinate. \Cref{fig:method:rope} illustrates this on a single plane: increasing the coordinate fans a vector around the circle at a fixed rate (left), while the phase enclosed between a rotated query and key—and hence their dot product—is set by the coordinate gap alone (right).

Nothing in this construction requires the coordinate to be an integer sequence position: the angle $p\theta_m$ in \cref{eq:method:rope_block} is well defined for any real $p$.
We therefore drive the rotations with raw pulse parameters rather than ordinal positions.
Setting $p_i$ to the \gls{toa} $t_i$ makes the relative phase in \cref{eq:method:rope} a function of the physical time gap $t_j - t_i$ between two pulses rather than of their ordinal distance—a natural fit for the irregularly sampled pulse trains of \cref{sec:method:data}.
Several coordinates can be encoded simultaneously by partitioning the $d_k/2$ planes into groups and driving each group with a different feature—here \gls{toa} on one group and \gls{aoa} on another—just as two-dimensional \gls{rope} for vision transformers assigns the two image axes to disjoint frequency groups \parencite{heo2024ropevit}.
The \gls{aoa} is a particularly natural candidate: it is itself an angle, so encoding it as a phase preserves its periodic structure.

This variant supplies an explicit relative encoding of time and direction, complementing rather than replacing the bias terms: the bias variants inject pairwise relationships additively into the logits, while \gls{rope} embeds coordinate differences in the geometry of the queries and keys.
It also revisits the choice of \cref{sec:method:arch} to omit a positional encoding and rely on \gls{toa} and \gls{aoa} as plain input features, testing whether an explicit relative encoding of these coordinates adds information beyond their presence in the input.
