\section{Implications}\label{sec:disc:implications}

If the task is deinterleaving alone, emitter-only Vanilla and joint RoPE-TOA sit at the same ceiling; there is no clear winner (\cref{tab:exp:ablation_em,tab:exp:attn_em_task}).
Emitter-only Bias is not a substitute (\cref{tab:exp:attn_em_task}).
If the task is modes alone, a mode-only encoder is slightly below joint Vanilla, and below joint RoPE-TOA and Bias (\cref{tab:exp:ablation_md,tab:exp:attn_md}).
Classical \gls{esm} pipelines analyze mode after deinterleaving, on each recovered train (\cref{sec:bg:elint_pipeline}).
Feature \gls{dbscan} already shows that the mode partition is the more separable of the two in the raw \gls{pdw} features (\cref{tab:exp:vanilla}), so a later mode stage on a well recovered train is the easier geometry.
An emitter-only encoder can in principle feed that second stage; a classical mode clusterer or classifier on those trains was not scored, so the single-task pipeline is not a measured alternative.
A sparse split such as \cref{fig:exp:feat_imbalance} is the caveat: deinterleaving is hard, and a recovered train with three pulses is a poor input to any later mode classifier.
Joint mode clustering on the mixed window does not remove that sparsity.
The joint loss is for the case where both partitions are required from the same interleaved window.
With RoPE-TOA it is not a compromise on emitters.
On Vanilla the unweighted sum still is.

If both partitions are required, standard self-attention is already enough for modes on this corpus and is not enough for the hardest emitter windows.
Putting \gls{toa} into the attention product, either as RoPE-TOA or as the pairwise bias, is what closes that gap.
The two mechanisms do the same job.
RoPE-TOA does it without extra weights and without a material change in encoder time.
The bias matches those scores and spends memory and milliseconds on pairwise maps.
RoPE-TOA is the practical choice of the two.
Using both at once does not help on this split, and it keeps the pairwise cost.
Incidence rotary encoding on the deinterleaving branch is not a substitute, once the Cartesian direction channels are already input features.

The clustering path at inference keeps the output open in form.
It does not, by itself, demonstrate that unseen modes would form their own clusters.
Until a held-out-modulation split is scored, operators should read the mode indices as a grouping of catalogue types that were present in training, not as a detector of new waveforms.
Emitter indices are window-local hypotheses. They still need a later association stage if the product is a track, not a window.

For representation learning more broadly, the useful Vanilla fact is the one in \cref{tab:exp:ablation_em,tab:exp:ablation_md}.
Two supervised contrastive terms with different positive-set scopes can share a trunk, and the trunk will trade one partition against the other.
That is expected once the two similarities conflict, and it is measurable.
A relative weight between the two terms, or detaching more of the trunk, is the obvious next control when both heads must stay close to their single-task ceilings.

The ethical limits in \cref{sec:intro:ethics} do not change with these scores.
The work is a clustering method on synthetic pulse features.
It is not an identification system, and it is not ready to be read as one.
