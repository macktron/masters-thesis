\section{Interpretation of Results}\label{sec:disc:interp}

The experiments separate two axes.
The first is which contrastive term is active, on a matched Vanilla encoder (\cref{sec:exp:ablation}).
The second is how time and incidence enter the attention product, under the joint objective (\cref{sec:exp:attention}).
This section follows that order.

\subsection{Joint Versus Single-Task Training}\label{sec:disc:joint}

The two contrastive terms pull on incompatible similarities in the shared trunk: pulses that should be close as modes may need to be far as emitters.
Emitter-only Vanilla already deinterleaves more cleanly than joint Vanilla (\cref{tab:exp:ablation_loss,fig:exp:kcorr_ablation_em}).
Mode-only matches Joint on modes (\cref{tab:exp:ablation_loss,fig:exp:kcorr_ablation_md}).

Under $\Loss_{\mathrm{joint}}$ the trunk yields to that conflict on the emitter side and keeps the mode side intact.
Joint training is still the only objective that trains both partitions in one run.
The leftover joint emitter errors sit in a minority of windows rather than on every window.
Those windows are mixtures in which frequency and pulse width are shared across platforms, of the kind drawn in \cref{fig:exp:feat_maxmode}.
Window $100$ of the original overlapping dump (test scenario $1752$) is the opposite case. The two emitters are already separated in incidence and in the scanning amplitude.

The attention variants were all trained jointly, so the emitter gains of RoPE-TOA and Bias in \cref{sec:disc:attention} are gains under that more demanding objective, not under a deinterleaving-only loss.
On RoPE-TOA the joint tax is absent: an emitter-only copy of that encoder matches the joint emitter $\ARI$ already reported in \cref{sec:exp:attention}.

\subsection{Physical Structure in Attention}\label{sec:disc:attention}

Mode clustering is already strong with standard self-attention.
The attention variants mainly move the emitter scores, and they do not all move them the same way.
The tail in \cref{fig:exp:hist_em} and the off-diagonal mass in \cref{fig:exp:kcorr_em} are the leftover joint emitter errors of \cref{sec:disc:joint}.
Every jointly trained model in \cref{fig:exp:emb_w100_em} recovers both partitions on that scenario-$1752$ window.

RoPE-TOA puts time into every attention product.
On this corpus that is enough to close the emitter tail, with no extra weights.
Inter-arrival structure is the cue that distinguishes pulse trains once RF and pulse width collide, and rotating queries and keys by window-local \gls{toa} makes that cue available in the inner product rather than only as one input channel.

RoPE-az spends the deinterleaving rotations on planar incidence instead.
Incidence is a useful input feature, as \cref{fig:exp:feat_w100_em} shows, but using it as the rotary coordinate of the deinterleaving branch does not beat rotating that branch by \gls{toa}.
The embeddings in \cref{fig:exp:emb_w100_em} agree with the table. RoPE-az still splits the two colors, and it does not tighten them the way RoPE-TOA does.
A likely reason is that the Cartesian incidence channels are already in the token. Rotary encoding of those channels restates a cue the encoder already has, while TOA rotary encoding restates a cue that standard attention otherwise has to learn from a single scalar.

The physical bias reaches the same emitter scores as RoPE-TOA by a more expensive route.
It adds a handful of learned scalars, not a second network.
What it adds at run time is a pairwise map of shape $h \times L \times L$ per layer.
With $h=8$ and $L=2000$, one such map is $128$~MB in fp32.
Twelve attention sublayers make about $1.5$~GB if the maps are all resident, and a training dump at batch size $4$ reports $30$~GB of forward and backward storage for Bias against $19$~GB for Vanilla.
That gap is the pairwise tensors, counted twice when gradients are kept.
At the evaluation batch size of one window the extra is a few gigabytes rather than tens, which matches the encoder time in \cref{tab:exp:efficiency}: $32$~ms instead of $1.6$~ms, while \gls{dbscan} stays near $55$~ms for every model.
The $32$~ms includes the uncached rebuild of the same $\lvert p_i-p_j\rvert$ matrices in every head of every sub-layer (\cref{sec:exp:results:efficiency}).
A cache would remove the rebuild.
It would not remove the subtraction from each head's logits, which is still quadratic in $L$.

The intended split of \cref{sec:method:attention:bias} was that deinterleaving would use incidence and that the mode branch would ignore it.
\Cref{tab:exp:bias_coef} does not show that split.
The largest-magnitude means sit on the mode branch, and they are incidence, not time.
Those table entries are averages over the eight heads and four layers of each stack, not a single shared coefficient.

Because the term is subtracted from the logits, a positive $\bar\lambda_{\mathrm{inc}}$ decreases attention as the incidence difference grows: pulses from different directions attend less.
That uses viewing geometry on the mode branch rather than ignoring it.
The Bias mode scores remain the tightest in \cref{tab:exp:attn_md}, so that penalty does not break mode clustering on this corpus.

The deinterleaving means are also positive, and of similar size on time and on incidence.
A positive $\bar\lambda_t$ there decreases attention as the \gls{toa} gap grows, so a pulse attends more strongly to its temporal neighbours than to pulses farther away in the interleaved stream.
Individual heads can differ in sign, as the $-0.832$ incidence head on the Bias emitter branch shows.
Those reversed heads raise attention as the coordinate gap grows.
Trunk means stay near zero, so most of the pairwise work is in the branches.

RoPE never builds those maps.
Its dumped parameter count matches Vanilla, and its encoder time stays within a couple of milliseconds of Vanilla.
The total-size figure printed for the RoPE summaries is an artifact of expanding rotation blocks in the layer dump, not a measured allocation.

Stacking the two time mechanisms does not add their gains.
RoPE-TOA+Bias pays the Bias encoder time and scores below Vanilla on emitters (\cref{tab:exp:attn_em,fig:exp:hist_em}).
The extra errors are splits (\cref{fig:exp:kcorr_em}).
Both devices already put a function of $\Delta t$ into the attention product, so the stacked logits can double-count time.
The stacked checkpoint still has a large positive mode incidence mean (\cref{tab:exp:bias_coef}), while its mode time mean changes sign relative to Bias.
Under subtraction, that sign change turns a temporal distance term into a temporal proximity term.
That is consistent with rotary encoding already carrying time and with the bias remaining an incidence penalty.
The tables give no reason to pay for both.

The accuracy of the bias is a statement about pairwise geometry.
A few scalars on $\lvert p_i-p_j\rvert$ recover the same emitter partition as rotating every query and key by time.
Vanilla has to learn that structure from a single \gls{toa} channel.
The differences themselves already carry it.
That points to a network whose layers read those delta matrices, rather than combining them with a learned query--key product.
Self-attention on a window is already mixing on a complete graph (\cref{sec:bg:transformer}).
The bias is already the Graphormer-style edge term of \cref{sec:method:attention:bias,sec:related:transformers_seq}.
A graph network whose edges carry $(\lvert\Delta t\rvert,\lvert\Delta\tilde{u}^{x}\rvert,\lvert\Delta\tilde{u}^{y}\rvert)$ would use that pairwise physics directly.
Whether it matches the scores, and whether it is cheaper once the differences are computed once, is not measured here.
