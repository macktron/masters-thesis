\section{Ablation Studies}\label{sec:exp:ablation}

This section isolates the joint objective of \cref{eq:method:loss_total} from the attention variants of \cref{sec:exp:results:attention}.
The runs use the Vanilla encoder of \cref{sec:method:arch}, so the only change is which contrastive term is active.
That matched dual-branch control is the comparison for RQ2.

The three objectives are:
\begin{description}
  \item[Joint] $\Loss = \Loss_{\mathrm{em}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{md}}\Loss_{\mathrm{md}}$ with $\lambda_{\mathrm{md}} = 1$, identical to Vanilla in \cref{sec:exp:results}.
  \item[Emitter-only] $\Loss = \Loss_{\mathrm{em}}$. The mode branch receives no direct gradient.
  \item[Mode-only] $\Loss = \Loss_{\mathrm{md}}$. The emitter branch receives no direct gradient.
\end{description}
Joint numbers in \cref{tab:exp:ablation_loss} are copied from the Vanilla row of \cref{tab:exp:attn_em,tab:exp:attn_md}, not from a second training run.
The single-task models use the same initialisation seed, the same splits, and the remaining hyperparameters of \cref{sec:exp:hparams}.

Both branches are decoded with \gls{dbscan} in every run, including the branch that was not trained.
The informative cells are therefore the emitter scores of Mode-only and the mode scores of Emitter-only.
They measure how much the shared trunk transfers to the unsupervised branch, versus collapse of that branch.

Count error is $\lvert\hat{K}-K\rvert$ on the emitter branch and $\lvert\hat{M}-M\rvert$ on the mode branch.

On the emitter branch, Emitter-only outperforms Joint.
Mean $\ARI$ rises from $0.944$ to $0.999$, the spread collapses, and the count error falls to the same range as RoPE-TOA and Bias in \cref{tab:exp:attn_em}.
Adding the mode term therefore costs deinterleaving accuracy on this backbone, at least at $\lambda_{\mathrm{md}}=1$.

The unpaid cost of dropping the mode term is the mode branch itself.
Under Emitter-only that branch still produces clusters, but they merge: homogeneity falls to $0.79$ while completeness stays at $0.999$, and the mean count error exceeds two modes per window.
The clusters are internally pure and too few.
Joint training is what keeps the mode partition usable.

RQ2, as stated, asks whether the joint objective outperforms a comparable transformer trained for deinterleaving alone.
On the emitter metrics it does not.
What it buys is a second partition that Emitter-only does not provide.
