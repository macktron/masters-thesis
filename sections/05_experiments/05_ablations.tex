\section{Ablation Studies}\label{sec:exp:ablation}

This section isolates the joint objective of \cref{eq:method:loss_total} from the attention mechanism.
The runs use the Vanilla encoder of \cref{sec:method:arch}, so the only change is which contrastive term is active.
That matched dual-branch control is the comparison for RQ2.
The jointly trained attention variants are compared afterwards in \cref{sec:exp:attention}.

The three objectives are:
\begin{description}
  \item[Joint] $\Loss_{\mathrm{joint}} = \Loss_{\mathrm{em}} + \Loss_{\mathrm{md}}$, identical to Vanilla in \cref{sec:exp:results:vanilla}.
  \item[Emitter-only] $\Loss_{\mathrm{em}}$. The mode branch receives no direct gradient.
  \item[Mode-only] $\Loss_{\mathrm{md}}$. The emitter branch receives no direct gradient.
\end{description}
Joint numbers in \cref{tab:exp:ablation_loss} are copied from the Vanilla rows of \cref{tab:exp:vanilla}, not from a second training run.
The single-task models use the same splits and the remaining hyperparameters of \cref{sec:exp:hparams}.

Both branches are decoded with \gls{dbscan} in every run, including the branch that was not trained.
The informative cells are therefore the emitter scores of Mode-only and the mode scores of Emitter-only.
They measure how much the shared trunk transfers to the unsupervised branch, versus collapse of that branch.

Count error is $\lvert\hat{K}-K\rvert$ on the emitter branch and $\lvert\hat{M}-M\rvert$ on the mode branch.

On the emitter branch, Emitter-only outperforms Joint.
Mean $\ARI$ rises from $0.948$ to $0.999$, the spread collapses, and the count error falls to $0.12\pm0.36$.
Adding the mode term therefore costs deinterleaving accuracy on this backbone.

The unpaid cost of dropping the mode term is the mode branch itself.
Under Emitter-only that branch still produces clusters, but they merge: homogeneity falls to $0.79$ while completeness stays at $0.998$, and the mean count error exceeds two modes per window.
The clusters are internally pure and too few.

Mode-only is the other single-task ceiling.
Its mode $\ARI$ is $0.983$, within rounding of Joint.
The untrained emitter branch splits rather than merges: homogeneity stays at $0.93$ while completeness falls to $0.70$, and the mean count error is $1.86\pm1.53$.
True emitters are broken into extra clusters.
Window $200$ (test scenario $1793$) is a local case: two emitters and four modes, with Mode-only recovering the emitters as four clusters ($\ARI=0.030$).

\Cref{fig:exp:emb_ablation} shows that geometry on the same window and on a crowded counterpart (window $500$, four emitters and twelve modes).
Joint keeps both partitions.
Emitter-only keeps emitters and merges modes that share a platform.
Mode-only keeps modes and splits emitters that occupy several modes.
Red frames mark the branch that received no gradient.

\Cref{fig:exp:kcorr_ablation_em,fig:exp:kcorr_ablation_md} show those count errors as heatmaps of estimated versus true cluster counts over the same $1168$ windows.
Each cell is a window count.
The diagonal is exact recovery.
Mass above the diagonal is extra clusters (splits).
Mass below it is missing clusters (merges).
The Joint panels are the Vanilla evaluation of \cref{sec:exp:results:vanilla}, not a second run.

On the emitter branch, Emitter-only sits on the diagonal.
Mode-only leaves most of its mass above it.
On the mode branch the pattern reverses: Mode-only stays with Joint, and Emitter-only sits below the diagonal.

RQ2, as stated, asks whether the joint objective outperforms a comparable transformer trained for deinterleaving alone.
On the emitter metrics it does not.
It also does not beat Mode-only on the mode metrics.
What it buys is that both partitions remain usable in the same run.
The next section asks whether physical structure in attention recovers the emitter accuracy of Emitter-only without dropping the mode term.
