\section{Joint versus Single-Task Objectives}\label{sec:exp:ablation}

This section compares the joint loss of \cref{eq:method:loss_total} with training on only one of the two contrastive terms.
That is the comparison for RQ2.
Every run uses the Vanilla encoder of \cref{sec:method:arch}.
Single-task runs keep the dual-branch layout so that width and depth match, the inactive branch is severed from the backward pass, so it does not affect the trained parameters and is not scored.

The three objectives are:
\begin{description}
  \item[Joint] $\Loss_{\mathrm{joint}} = \Loss_{\mathrm{em}} + \Loss_{\mathrm{md}}$, identical to Vanilla in \cref{sec:exp:results:vanilla}. Both branches are scored.
  \item[Emitter-only] $\Loss_{\mathrm{em}}$. The emitter branch is scored.
  \item[Mode-only] $\Loss_{\mathrm{md}}$. The mode branch is scored.
\end{description}

Joint numbers in \cref{tab:exp:ablation_loss} are copied from the Vanilla rows of \cref{tab:exp:vanilla}, not from a second training run.
The single-task models use the same splits and the remaining hyperparameters of \cref{sec:exp:hparams}, except $\varepsilon$, which is selected independently on the validation grid (\cref{tab:exp:hparams_attn}).

\begin{table}[tbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Joint versus single-task objectives on the Vanilla encoder. Single-task rows report the active branch only.}
  \label{tab:exp:ablation_loss}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}llccccc@{}}
    \toprule
    Objective & Branch & $\ARI$ & $\AMI$ & Hom. & Comp. & Count error \\
    \midrule
    Joint         & Emitter & $0.948\pm0.176$ & $0.957\pm0.138$ & $0.989\pm0.052$ & $0.951\pm0.166$ & $0.25\pm0.49$ \\
    Joint         & Mode    & $0.985\pm0.107$ & $0.986\pm0.102$ & $0.994\pm0.027$ & $0.988\pm0.103$ & $0.23\pm0.50$ \\
    Emitter-only  & Emitter & $0.999\pm0.012$ & $0.997\pm0.013$ & $0.995\pm0.021$ & $0.999\pm0.009$ & $0.12\pm0.36$ \\
    Mode-only     & Mode    & $0.983\pm0.110$ & $0.984\pm0.103$ & $0.992\pm0.036$ & $0.988\pm0.102$ & $0.24\pm0.56$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

On the emitter branch, Emitter-only outperforms Joint.
Mean $\ARI$ rises from $0.948$ to $0.999$, the spread collapses, and the count error falls to $0.12\pm0.36$.
Adding the mode term therefore costs deinterleaving performance on this backbone.

Mode-only is the other single-task ceiling.
Its mode $\ARI$ is $0.983$, within rounding of Joint.

\Cref{fig:exp:emb_ablation} shows UMAP projections of the active-branch embeddings on two test windows from different scenarios.
Window $200$ has two emitters and four modes; window $500$ has four emitters and twelve modes.
Joint panels are both heads.
Each single-task panel is the head that received the loss.

The diagonal is exact recovery.

On the emitter branch, Emitter-only sits on the diagonal more tightly than Joint.
On the mode branch, Mode-only stays with Joint.

RQ2, as stated, asks whether the joint objective outperforms a comparable transformer trained for deinterleaving alone.
On the emitter metrics it does not.
It also does not beat Mode-only on the mode metrics.
What it buys is that both partitions are trained in the same run.
The next section asks whether physical structure in attention recovers the emitter accuracy of Emitter-only without dropping the mode term.
