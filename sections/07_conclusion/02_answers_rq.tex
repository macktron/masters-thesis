\section{Answers to the Research Questions}\label{sec:concl:answers}

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1]
  Yes, on this corpus.
  The Vanilla encoder of \cref{tab:exp:attn_em,tab:exp:attn_md} deinterleaves pulses and recovers modes by clustering a bounded window of \glspl{pdw}.
  Mode agreement is high on almost every window. Emitter agreement is high on most windows and fails on a minority of crowded mixtures.

  \item[RQ2]
  Not on the emitter task.
  A Vanilla encoder trained only on $\Loss_{\mathrm{em}}$ outperforms joint Vanilla on emitter $\ARI$ (\cref{tab:exp:ablation_loss}).
  The joint objective is what prevents the mode branch from collapsing by merging.

  \item[RQ3]
  Yes for time, with a cost that depends on how time is injected.
  RoPE-TOA and the physical bias both remove the Vanilla emitter tail (\cref{tab:exp:attn_em,fig:exp:hist_em}).
  Rotary encoding of planar incidence on the deinterleaving branch improves on Vanilla and does not match TOA-only rotary encoding.
  Mode scores move much less.
  The bias matches RoPE-TOA on accuracy and spends extra memory and encoder time on pairwise maps (\cref{tab:exp:efficiency,sec:disc:interp}).
\end{description}
