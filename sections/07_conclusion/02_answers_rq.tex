\section{Answers to the Research Questions}\label{sec:concl:answers}

\begin{description}
  \item[RQ1]
  \emph{Given a bounded window of \glspl{pdw}, can a transformer encoder deinterleave pulses and recover operating modes by clustering?}

  Yes, on this corpus.
  The Vanilla encoder of \cref{tab:exp:vanilla} deinterleaves pulses and recovers modes by clustering a bounded window of \glspl{pdw}.
  Mode agreement is high on almost every window. Emitter agreement is high on most windows and fails on a minority of crowded mixtures.
  Both scores sit well above Feature \gls{dbscan} on the same test split.

  \item[RQ2]
  \emph{Does a joint objective that couples deinterleaving with mode clustering outperform a comparable transformer trained for deinterleaving alone?}

  The joint encoder is at par once time is in attention, not under Vanilla joint training.
  Joint RoPE-TOA and Bias match or slightly exceed deinterleaving-only training on emitters and are slightly higher on modes than mode-only Vanilla (\cref{tab:exp:attn_em_task,tab:exp:ablation_loss,tab:exp:attn_md}).
  The margins are small, and there is no clear winner between emitter-only Vanilla and joint RoPE-TOA on deinterleaving.
  A fairer single-task comparison would run a classical mode clusterer or classifier on the trains of an emitter-only encoder, which was not scored.
  On Vanilla attention the joint loss still costs emitters (\cref{tab:exp:ablation_loss}).

  \item[RQ3]
  \emph{Do a pairwise physical attention bias and rotary positional encoding on pulse coordinates improve clustering relative to standard multi-head attention?}

  Yes for time, with a cost that depends on how time is injected.
  RoPE-TOA and the physical bias both remove the Vanilla emitter tail (\cref{tab:exp:attn_em,fig:exp:hist_em}).
  Stacking them does not: RoPE-TOA+Bias is slower than Vanilla and worse on emitters.
  Rotary encoding of planar incidence on the deinterleaving branch improves on Vanilla and does not match TOA-only rotary encoding.
  Mode scores move much less.
  The two mechanisms do the same job; the bias is the quadratic version, and RoPE-TOA is cheaper (\cref{tab:exp:efficiency,sec:disc:attention}).
\end{description}
