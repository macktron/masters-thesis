\section{Summary}\label{sec:concl:summary}

The thesis addresses three questions.
RQ1 asks whether, given a bounded window of \glspl{pdw}, a transformer encoder can deinterleave pulses and recover operating modes by clustering.
RQ2 asks whether a joint objective that couples deinterleaving with mode clustering outperforms a comparable transformer trained for deinterleaving alone.
RQ3 asks whether a pairwise physical attention bias and rotary positional encoding on pulse coordinates improve clustering relative to standard multi-head attention.

The encoder shares a trunk, splits into two branches, and is trained with two supervised contrastive losses that differ only in the scope of their positive sets: recording-local emitter identifiers and global mode labels.
Inference clusters each branch with \gls{dbscan}. No catalogue softmax is used.

On the synthetic test split, standard self-attention already recovers both partitions on most windows.
Rotary encoding of window-local \gls{toa}, and a pairwise physical bias on the same coordinates, close the remaining emitter errors.
They inject time into the same product; the bias is quadratic in the window length, and RoPE-TOA is cheaper.
Using both together does not.
A joint RoPE-TOA or Bias encoder is at par with deinterleaving-only training on emitters, and slightly higher on modes than a mode-only encoder even under standard attention.
The margins are small; there is no clear winner between emitter-only Vanilla and joint RoPE-TOA on deinterleaving.
A later mode stage on the trains of a deinterleaving-only encoder was not scored.
On standard self-attention the joint loss still costs emitters.
